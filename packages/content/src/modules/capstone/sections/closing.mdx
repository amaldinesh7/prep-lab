## What you can claim now

The reason a capstone matters is the answers it lets you give in interviews and architecture reviews. After shipping this, you can speak honestly to:

- **"How would you handle real-time collaboration?"** You've shipped it. You can compare Yjs to Replicache to Convex without hand-waving.
- **"What does offline-first cost?"** You've measured it. You know the service worker caches the wrong thing if you don't tell it not to.
- **"How do you integrate AI agents into an existing product?"** You've architected it. Agent-as-peer is a non-obvious pattern that you've actually used.
- **"How do you keep a SPA's bundle small?"** You've fought it. You know where Yjs goes on the route map.

That's the difference between reading the curriculum and shipping the capstone. The first gives you vocabulary. The second gives you opinions.

## Where to go next

If you've gotten this far, three concrete next steps are worth considering, in roughly increasing scope:

1. **Use the app for a week.** Track your real tasks in it. You'll find a UX paper cut every hour. Fix three. The discipline of dogfooding is its own module.
2. **Pick one stretch goal and ship it.** WebRTC, mobile, desktop, or the observability dashboard. Going from v1 to v1.1 with a serious feature is where you internalize the patterns.
3. **Apply one pattern at work.** The next time your team builds something with a "two tabs disagree" failure mode, propose Yjs. Bring the capstone to the design review. The architecture you've shipped is the case study.

There's no eleventh module after this. The curriculum ends here on purpose. The next thing you build should be in the wild — at work, for users, with stakes.

**Remember this:** you didn't finish a course. You shipped a tool. Keep treating it like one.
